# Reading order: 22 downloaded PDFs

Ordered Sun to Earth, orientation first. Sequencing and the notes below are my
editorial judgment from each paper's ADS title, abstract, venue and citation
count. I have not read the PDF bodies, so treat the notes as routing, not as
findings.

Files are in `users/cayoung/workspace/data/arxiv_<id>.pdf`.


## Start here: the whole event in two papers

1. **The Solar and Geomagnetic Storms in 2024 May: A Flash Data Report**  
   Hayakawa, Hisashi (2025), *The Astrophysical Journal* · 156 cites · `arxiv_2407.07665.pdf`  
   Hayakawa's flash data report is the reference sheet for the whole event: AR growth 113->2761 millionths, the flare list, Dst, auroral extent. Read it first and keep it open while you read everything else.
2. **Interplanetary Causes and Impacts of the 2024 May Superstorm on the Geosphere: An Overview**  
   Hajra, Rajkumar (2024), *The Astrophysical Journal* · 55 cites · `arxiv_2408.14799.pdf`  
   Tsurutani-style interplanetary overview. Takes you from the L1 signature to the geosphere response in one pass, including the SI+ 88 nT and the three-step storm structure. The best single map of the chain.

## The Sun: why AR 13664 was capable of this

3. **Magnetic Field Evolution of the Solar Active Region 13664**  
   Jarolim, Robert (2024), *The Astrophysical Journal Letters* · 33 cites · `arxiv_2409.08124.pdf`  
   Magnetic field evolution of AR 13664. The flux-emergence story from about May 7, and the context for 12 X-class flares.
4. **Analyzing the Sequence of Phases Leading to the Formation of the Active Region 13664, with Potential Carrington-like Characteristics**  
   Romano, P. (2024), *The Astrophysical Journal Letters* · 21 cites · `arxiv_2409.04408.pdf`  
   How the region assembled, phase by phase, with the explicit Carrington-comparison framing.
5. **Deconstructing the Properties of Solar Super Active Region 13664 in the Context of the Historic Geomagnetic Storm of 2024 May 10─11**  
   Jaswal, Priyansh (2025), *The Astrophysical Journal* · 18 cites · `arxiv_2409.14752.pdf`  
   Deconstructs what makes a super active region, placing 13664 against the SAR population rather than treating it as a one-off.
6. **The Magnetic Topology of AR13664 Leading to Its First Halo CME**  
   MacTaggart, D. (2025), *Journal of Geophysical Research (Space Physics)* · 10 cites · `arxiv_2410.15964.pdf`  
   Magnetic topology behind the FIRST halo CME. Read after the two above; it assumes the field evolution.
7. **Various Features of the X-class White-light Flares in Super Active Region NOAA 13664**  
   Li, Ying (2024), *The Astrophysical Journal Letters* · 24 cites · `arxiv_2408.05725.pdf`  
   The X-class white-light flares themselves, from ASO-S/WST. Observational detail on the radiative output.
8. **Exploring the Origin of Multi-Periodic Pulsations During a White-Light Flare**  
   Li, Dong (2025), *Journal of Geophysical Research (Space Physics)* · 11 cites · `arxiv_2504.08321.pdf`  
   Narrow but nice: quasi-periodic pulsations in the X4.0 flare on May 10. Optional unless you care about flare physics.

## Corona to L1: the CMEs and their pileup

9. **A Pileup of Coronal Mass Ejections Produced the Largest Geomagnetic Storm in Two Decades**  
   Liu, Ying D. (2024), *The Astrophysical Journal Letters* · 35 cites · `arxiv_2409.11492.pdf`  
   The key propagation paper. Multipoint imaging plus in situ showing successive halos piling up, which is the mechanism for Dst -412 nT. Read right after the overviews.
10. **Unveiling key factors in solar eruptions leading to the solar superstorm in 2024 May**  
   Wang, Rui (2024), *Astronomy and Astrophysics* · 16 cites · `arxiv_2410.00891.pdf`  
   Which factors in the eruptions actually mattered, from the interacting-CME perspective.
11. **Observation and modeling of complex transient structure in heliosphere followed by geomagnetic storm on May 10─11, 2024**  
   Rodkin, Denis (2025), *Advances in Space Research* · 5 cites · `arxiv_2501.07160.pdf`  
   Modeling the complex transient structure that reached Earth. Pairs directly with the pileup paper.

## Particles

12. **The High-Energy Protons of the Ground Level Enhancement (GLE74) Event on 11 May 2024**  
   Papaioannou, A. (2025), *Solar Physics* · 11 cites · `arxiv_2505.09180.pdf`  
   GLE74 on 11 May: neutron monitor reconstruction and the high-energy proton spectrum. The radiation-storm anchor.
13. **MEMPSEP-I. Forecasting the Probability of Solar Energetic Particle Event Occurrence Using a Multivariate Ensemble of Convolutional Neural Networks**  
   Chatterjee, Subhamoy (2024), *Space Weather* · 9 cites · `arxiv_2309.14570.pdf`  
   MEMPSEP-I, SEP occurrence forecasting with a CNN ensemble. Method paper, not event-specific; read only if you want the forecasting angle.
14. **CORHEL-CME: An Interactive Tool For Modeling Solar Eruptions**  
   Linker, Jon A. (2024), *Journal of Physics Conference Series* · 14 cites · `arxiv_2311.03596.pdf`  
   CORHEL-CME modeling tool. Also a method paper. Same caveat.

## Geospace: thermosphere and drag

15. **Evidence of Potential Thermospheric Overcooling During the May 2024 Geomagnetic Superstorm**  
   Ranjan, Alok Kumar (2024), *Journal of Geophysical Research (Space Physics)* · 16 cites · `arxiv_2411.14071.pdf`  
   Thermospheric overcooling via NO infrared emission. The energetics counterweight to the heating story.
16. **Satellite Drag Analysis During the May 2024 Gannon Geomagnetic Storm**  
   Parker, William E. (2024), *Journal of Spacecraft and Rockets* · 58 cites · `arxiv_2406.08617.pdf`  
   Satellite drag analysis across the storm. The operational consequence, written for the LEO-constellation era.
17. **Loss of 12 Starlink Satellites Due to Pre-conditioning of Intense Space Weather Activity Surrounding the Extreme Geomagnetic Storm of 10 May 2024**  
   Ashruf, Ayisha M (2024), *arXiv e-prints* · 10 cites · `arxiv_2410.16254.pdf`  
   Loss of 12 Starlink satellites, traced through TLE-derived orbital decay. Concrete case study behind the drag paper.
18. **Tracking reentries of Starlink satellites during the rising phase of solar cycle 25**  
   Oliveira, Denny M. (2025), *Frontiers in Astronomy and Space Sciences* · 18 cites · `arxiv_2505.13752.pdf`  
   Starlink reentries across the rising phase of cycle 25. Puts May 2024 in a multi-year trend.
19. **The 10 October 2024 geomagnetic storm may have caused the premature reentry of a Starlink satellite**  
   Oliveira, Denny M. (2025), *Frontiers in Astronomy and Space Sciences* · 25 cites · `arxiv_2411.01654.pdf`  
   October 2024 storm, not May. Included because it is the natural comparison case for the reentry question.

## Ground effects

20. **GIC-Related Observations During the May 2024 Geomagnetic Storm in the United States**  
   Wilkerson, L. A. (2026), *Space Weather* · 4 cites · `arxiv_2507.07009.pdf`  
   GIC observations across US power grids during the storm. The best-grounded ground-effects paper in this set.
21. **A Reproducible Method for Mapping Electricity Transmission Infrastructure for Space Weather Risk Assessment**  
   Bor, Dennies K. (2024), *arXiv e-prints* · 3 cites · `arxiv_2412.17685.pdf`  
   Method for mapping transmission infrastructure for GIC risk. Infrastructure-data paper, read only if you want to model GICs yourself.

## Elsewhere in the solar system

22. **Impacting the dayside Martian ionosphere from above and below: Effects of the impact of CIRs and ICMEs close to aphelion (April 2021) and during dust storms (June/July 2022) seen with MAVEN ROSE**  
   Felici, Marianna (2024), *Icarus* · 8 cites · `arxiv_2404.14959.pdf`  
   Martian ionosphere response to CIRs and ICMEs, from MAVEN ROSE. Different events (2021-2022), not May 2024. Kept because it is the comparative-planet angle; skip if you want Earth only.
